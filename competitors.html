The Finding That Matters Most
This is the answer to "who actually gets installs regularly"

Nobody in this market reliably buys qualified install leads. What they have instead is a service base — Getzschman since 1960, Burton since 1989, BLT for over forty years — and a tune-up or membership that puts them inside a customer's house on a schedule.

When a fifteen-year-old furnace finally fails, the firm that has been servicing it is the firm quoting the replacement. That is not a lead source. It is an install pipeline they own, and it compounds every year.

You already described this mechanism yourself when you said repairs turn into installs. The difference is that they have built a product around it and you have not.

Forty years of trust and almost no digital position

BLT is the incumbent in Fremont itself and is measuring nothing, marking up nothing, and publishing six pages. They will beat you on reputation for years. They will not beat you on being findable, and they cannot retarget anybody.

The Omaha firms are the harder problem — Burton runs GA4 and Tag Manager with HVAC schema and a membership, and Controlled Comfort has 71 pages, a Meta pixel and the most complete markup of anyone here.

Where You Win, And It Is Not Where You Think
Nobody here is positioned on anything except age
  • Every one of these firms sells "we have been here a long time". 1951, 1960, 1964, 1989, forty years. That is the entire proposition on offer in this market.
  • Not one of them is speaking to the fear you named your company after. Nobody is saying "get a second opinion before you sign". You own that, and the name says it before anyone reads a word.
  • You cannot out-age 1960 and you never will. So stop competing there. A homeowner holding a frightening quote is not asking who is oldest — they are asking who is honest.
  • Age is also a weakness. A sixty-five-year-old firm is exactly who people are afraid is upselling them, and none of them can adopt your position without contradicting their own.
What We Would Do About It
  • Launch a maintenance agreement. It is the single biggest structural gap between you and every established firm here, and it is the mechanism that turns repairs into installs on a schedule rather than by luck.
  • Put the second-opinion promise on every page, not just in the company name. It is the only real position in this market and nobody can copy it.
  • Match the table stakes first — tappable phone, a form on every service page, HVAC business schema with hours. Three competitors have all of it; you have none of it.
  • Then measure. Two of these firms are tracking and one is retargeting. Until you are, you cannot tell which of them you are actually losing to.

They have sixty-five years and a service book. You have the only honest position in the market.

The service book is buildable. Your position is not copyable. Build the first before they notice the second.

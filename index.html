First — The Thing You Have That Money Cannot Buy
Your positioning is the sharpest in your market, and it is doing almost nothing
  • The company name is the sales pitch. "Call Before You Sign" names the exact fear every homeowner has when an HVAC salesman is standing in their basement with a quote. Nobody else in Nebraska is doing that.
  • "Second opinion" appears 23 times across your site. That is a genuine wedge — you are selling against the industry's own reputation rather than against another contractor.
  • Twelve years in the trade before you started this, and you are licensed, bonded and insured.
  • You were featured on WOWT's Omaha Everyday. Local television coverage. Most contractors never get that.

That last one does not appear anywhere on your website. Neither do your reviews. The strongest assets you own are invisible to the people deciding whether to call you.

Your AC repair page has no way to start a job

Somebody searching "AC repair Fremont" in July lands on that page, reads 547 words, and finds no form and no tappable number. They have to navigate to a separate contact page to do anything at all. Most will not.

HVAC is an emergency trade. The gap between "I need someone now" and "I have called someone" should be one tap. Right now it is three steps and a memory test.

Finding 2 — You Have Two Copies Of The Same Page, Competing With Each Other
/ac-repair 547 words /ac-repair-1 547 words Word-for-word overlap: 100.0% Title tag: identical on both Both published in your sitemap: yes
This is a Wix duplicate that never got deleted

It happens when a page is duplicated for editing and the copy is left behind. Google now has two identical pages with the same title competing for the same search, and has to pick one — or trust both less.

It is a five-minute fix: delete one, or redirect it to the other. It is also a signal worth reading — nobody has looked at this site's structure since it went up.

Finding 4 — Google Does Not Know You Are An HVAC Business

This is the markup that ties a website to a Google Business Profile

Your site tells Google it is a website. It does not tell Google it is a heating and cooling company in Fremont, Nebraska, with these hours, these services and these answers. A competitor sixty miles away is telling Google all of it.

You already have a written FAQ page. Marking it up properly is a configuration job, not a writing job, and it is how those questions become eligible to appear directly in search results.

And what is already right, which is more than it sounds
  • Your page titles are properly written — "AC Repair, Installation and Diagnostics in Fremont, NE" is exactly the right shape. Most small contractors get this wrong.
  • You have a real FAQ and a blog, which is more than most firms your age bother with.
  • The service pages exist and read well. They are thin, not bad.

You are not going to out-age a firm founded in 1960. You do not need to. You need to be findable in Fremont and Dodge County, and be the obvious choice for anyone who has just been handed a frightening quote.

What We Would Do, In Order
  • Make the phone tappable on every page and put a form on every service page. First day's work, costs nothing, and until it is done nothing else can be judged.
  • Put the meter on — GA4, a pixel and call tracking — so within two weeks you can see arrivals and calls yourself rather than take anyone's word for it.
  • Delete the duplicate AC repair page and mark up the site as an HVAC business with hours, services and your existing FAQ.
  • Put your proof on the page. The WOWT feature, your reviews, licensed and bonded and insured, and the second-opinion promise — stated on every page, not buried.
  • Build the towns — Fremont, Blair, and the Omaha suburbs you actually serve — then spend on getting in front of people, with a number you can check.

You built the business around telling homeowners to get a second opinion.

This is ours, on the website. The idea is right. The plumbing underneath it is not built yet.

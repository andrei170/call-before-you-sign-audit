A maintenance plan is not a maintenance product. It is an install pipeline.

Here is the mechanism, in order:

  • A repair is the need. Somebody has no heat, they ring, you fix it. That is a transaction and it ends there.
  • A plan turns that transaction into a relationship. They sign up for two visits a year, usually for a small monthly fee or an annual one.
  • Twice a year, your technician is standing in front of that system. He sees the age of it, the efficiency, the parts that are starting to go.
  • He is the one who says “this has two winters left in it.” Not an advert. A man the homeowner already trusts, looking at their actual furnace.
  • When it fails — or before it fails — you get the install. Not a bid against three other companies. The call.

That is what “qualified installs, regularly” actually looks like. It is not a lead source. It is a stock of relationships that produces installs on a predictable curve, because every heating system in America is dying at a known rate.

Why This Answers Your Objection Rather Than Dodging It
You said you do not think anyone gets qualified installs regularly

If you mean by advertising for installs — you are right, and we would not sell you that. Install adverts reach people who are not in the market, and the few who respond are price-shopping three bids. That is the worst work in the trade.

What we are describing is different in one specific way: the customer is already yours. You are not finding them. You already met them on a repair. The plan simply stops you losing them between the repair and the replacement.

And you can predict it. If a plan member's system is fourteen years old, you know roughly when that conversation happens. That is the part that makes it regular — not the marketing, the arithmetic.

What This Means For The Advertising
It changes what we would advertise for, not whether
What we would NOT advertise for: "new furnace" "AC installation" "system replacement" — not a need, price-shopped, three bids What we WOULD advertise for: "furnace repair" "no heat" "AC not working" "emergency HVAC" "furnace tune up" — urgent, one call, they book the first firm that answers

Repairs are the need, exactly as you said. So we buy the repairs.

The plan is what we offer at the end of the repair, and the installs come out of the plan twelve, twenty-four or thirty-six months later. You are not buying installs. You are buying the front of a pipeline you currently do not have.

Worth saying plainly: this is a slower model than "run ads, get installs". The first year mostly builds the stock. We would rather tell you that now than in month three.

Two Things On Your Website That Sit In The Way
/ac-repair and /ac-repair-1 two URLs, 100% identical content Tappable phone numbers across 8 pages: 0 Enquiry forms: 1
The second one matters more than it looks

You are buying repair searches. A repair search is made by somebody whose heat has just gone out, on a phone, usually standing next to the thing that broke.

Your number is not tappable on any of your eight pages. They have to memorise it, leave your site and type it in. In an emergency, a good proportion will simply ring the next result instead.

That is a five-minute change, and until it is made, every dollar spent on repair advertising leaks at the last step.

And the duplicate page — /ac-repair and /ac-repair-1 are the same page twice. Pick one, redirect the other. It splits whatever ranking either one has earned.

What We Would Do, In Order
  • Make the phone number tappable on every page. Before anything is spent. It is the last step of every repair enquiry you will ever buy.
  • Fix the duplicate AC repair page. One URL, one redirect.
  • Build the maintenance plan — the offer, the price, the two visits, what is included. You are the only firm in this comparison without one, and it is the whole mechanism.
  • Put the plan in front of every repair customer, at the end of the job, as part of the technician's normal close.
  • Then advertise for repairs — no heat, AC not working, emergency. Urgent, high intent, and they book whoever answers.
  • Track the plan count monthly. Not installs, not leads. Plan members is the number that predicts your install revenue two years out, and it is the one that tells us whether this is working.

Nobody advertises their way to qualified installs. They earn them twice a year, in somebody's basement.

Your three biggest competitors worked that out. Two of them did it before 1990.
